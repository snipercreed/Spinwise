200 learners walk into Spinwise

A simulated field test of the app as it stands today: who gets in, who stays, what trips people up, and where to go next.

How to read this. MEASURED means I ran the app's real code (importer, wheel, rewards, rendering) against generated data. MODELLED means the number depends on my assumptions about human behaviour, such as how often Claude's output is malformed and how fast people drift away. Simulated people are not real people, so treat absolute retention as illustrative. The ranking of problems and the relative size of each fix are the useful part. Nothing here was run on a physical phone.

Spinwise's core loop is good and fast. Its weak points are all at the edges: getting data in, rewarding effort fairly, and readability on the more colourful themes. These are fixable, and fixing the first one is worth more than any new feature.

Who the 200 were

Eleven archetypes, each with its own subjects, spec size, approach to the wheel, and tolerance for friction. They drew on a pool of 24 subjects, from 8-topic manual lists to 1,800-topic medical specs.

1 · Getting in (onboarding)

Where people drop out MODELLED

Of the 145 who tried the Claude route, 49 failed the first import and 26 gave up. Of the 55 who typed topics in by hand, 37 abandoned during setup. That is a 69% cliff, because there is no way to paste a list in.

2 · Staying (retention)

Share of all 200 still active on day N MODELLED

TodayForgiving importerRemindersFixes + reminders + easier manual entry

The model uses a steep week-one drop-off, a flatter tail, softening from streaks and rewards, and extra drop-off after a failed import or lots of wheel skipping. By day 30, 21.5% of everyone is still active today, and 37% would be with the combined fixes. Almost all of that gain comes from getting more people through the door.

Biggest single lever

A forgiving importer: onboarded users go from 68.5% → 81.5% of everyone. That beats reminders (+7 pts of onboarded users still active at day 30) because you can't retain someone who never started.

Who sticks, who doesn't

Steady A-level and medical-spec users keep going (many active days, long streaks). Casual tryers leave in a few days whatever you do (11.8% reach day 7). Manual-entry users mostly never finish setup. Perfectionists run out of things to do: they reach about 89% revised with near-100% coverage.

3 · Findings, ranked

4 · What's working

  • The core loop is fast. Spin, log, done is about 3 taps. Every screen renders in under 25 ms of JavaScript even at 1,800 topics. MEASURED
  • No layout breakage at 375 px: zero horizontal overflow on all six screens, including a 150-character topic title and a 12-subject bar. MEASURED
  • Importing is idempotent. Re-pasting the same spec never duplicates topics, and progress is preserved on merge.
  • Multi-subject colour coding let the "all subjects" view stay readable across 24 different subjects.
  • Reduced-motion and the Off switch are respected, which is rare and matters for accessibility.
  • Wheel weighting favours what needs work: weak and due topics are weighted up, and a topic revised today is weighted down by design.

5 · Economy check MEASURED

Too fast, and decoupled from effort

One revision a day earns about 60 ✦ per day, so the whole 2,540 ✦ catalogue is gone in roughly 42 days. In the first week that is 392 ✦: enough for a 300 ✦ theme by day 7. The flat +25 daily-goal bonus is the cause, since it pays every day regardless of how much was studied.

Easy to farm

Logging the same topic repeatedly pays about 13 ✦ each time with no limit, so 194 taps (a few minutes) buys everything. Resetting and re-logging a topic earns 485 ✦ in 20 rounds. In the simulation, "collector" users earned about 8× more than honest users (59.6k vs 7.2k ✦ total).

6 · Performance, layout and accessibility MEASURED

These are JavaScript times in a desktop browser. The real risk on a phone is paint cost: frosted-glass blur over thousands of DOM nodes (11,368 on the Topics screen at 1,800 topics, since every topic is rendered at once). Worth testing on your device with a big spec.

Contrast (WCAG AA = 4.5:1)

Touch and text size

  • Subject chips, wallet and most secondary buttons are 35–38 px tall (Android guideline: 48).
  • Labels at 10–11 px on Today, Topics, Progress and Shop.
  • The wheel is a canvas, so screen readers cannot see which topic won.
  • Muted text over bright backdrops drops to about 3.1–4.0:1.

7 · Where to go next

Five directions. They stack, but order matters.

A · Fix the foundation RECOMMENDED FIRST

Make getting data in forgiving, make rewards fair, make everything readable.

  • Smart importer: repair or recover broken JSON (truncated, trailing commas, curly quotes), keep every complete topic from a cut-off reply, a preview screen before import, clear error messages, never merge distinct topics that share a title.
  • Bulk add by paste for the no-Claude route.
  • Economy: diminishing returns per topic per day, scale the daily bonus with goal size, cap farmable income, slow the catalogue to about 3–4 months of honest use.
  • Readability pass: auto-pick dark or light text on each hero gradient, 48 px touch targets, 12 px minimum text.

Modelled effect: setup success 68% → 82–87%, and it removes the two trust-breaking bugs (silent data loss, inflated shop).

B · Make it stick

Daily reminders (Android notifications), a capped Today queue instead of an unbounded "due" count (engaged users reach about 98 due topics, p90 215), streak freeze, a weekly recap you can paste to Claude. Modelled effect: about +7 pts of onboarded users still active at day 30.

C · Make it feel premium

The realistic liquid glass, a Glass quality setting, wheel lens, richer haptics. Improves delight and screenshots, but does not move retention in the model. Do it after A, and test performance on your phone first.

D · Make it teach, not just track

Today's "Log revision" is self-reported. Add a 60-second self-test per topic (Claude generates questions you paste in), time spent, past-paper marks, and an exam-countdown planner that spreads topics across the days left. This is the real differentiator, and it needs the most design.

E · Open it up to other people

Cloud sync across devices, sharing a checklist with a friend, a Play Store release. Only worth it if you want other users. It needs a backend, accounts, and privacy work.

My recommendation: A, then B, then C, with D as the long-term bet. The simulation says the next ten points of success come from setup, not from polish. And before any of it, spend one evening watching three real people try to set the app up. That will reveal things no simulation can.
